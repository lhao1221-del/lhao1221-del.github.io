Research

I work on analog computing architectures built from voltage-controlled oscillators. Phase and frequency carry the signal, so the circuits scale with CMOS technology instead of fighting its shrinking voltage headroom. Alongside that, I design dynamic-element-matching DACs, transimpedance amplifiers, and optical driver circuits.

  • Analog and mixed-signal design
  • VCO-based analog computing
  • VCO-based quantum computing circuits
  • DEM DACs
  • VCO-based low-pass filters
  • TIA and driver circuits

Chips

Ring-oscillator and memristor linear-equation solver Measured

University at Buffalo, NSF project, 65-nm TSMC CMOS

An 8 × 8 analog linear-equation system built from ring VCOs and memristors. It performs 4 × 4 matrix inversion for massive-MIMO detection. These are measured silicon results:

2 µssolution latency
0.4 mWpower
0.0146 mm²area
8-bitaccuracy

12-qubit quantum computing emulator Pre-tape-out

University at Buffalo, 65-nm TSMC CMOS

A VCO and memristor chip that runs Grover's search and the quantum Fourier transform. It has been simulated in Cadence Virtuoso and is planned for tape-out.

Ring-VCO integrator and DEM DAC Fabricated

University at Buffalo, 65-nm TSMC CMOS

A ring-VCO-based integrator, now fabricated, and a dynamic-element-matching DAC modeled in Simulink and Virtuoso.

Silicon optoelectronic interface driver for optical computing Fabricated

Xi'an Jiaotong University, 180-nm TSMC CMOS, 2022–2024

An eight-channel Mach-Zehnder modulator driver with eight-segment MZM optical paths. It takes 1 Gb/s input and performs up to 8-bit optoelectronic digital-to-analog conversion.

Pulsed time-of-flight LiDAR receiver front end In production

Xi'an Jiaotong University, 180-nm TSMC CMOS, 2021–2022

A folded, stacked in-channel structure reuses current for higher power efficiency and integrates the low-power supply with the front end. The chip is now in commercial production.
